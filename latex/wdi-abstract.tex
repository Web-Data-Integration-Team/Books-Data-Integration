\begin{abstract}
We assess the feasibility of integrating Goodreads, DBpedia, and Amazon book data into a common schema. The sources contain 52,478, 20,981, and 40,000 records, respectively. A blocking key formed from normalized title and first-author surname yields 10,397 distinct keys in at least two datasets and 94,823 distinct keys in the union. A manual review labels 60 of 60 sampled candidate pairs as matches (20 per dataset pair). These are key-based estimates, not a materialized set of confirmed works. ISBN is reserved for gold-standard evaluation and is not used for matching. The target schema contains \texttt{title}, \texttt{authors}, \texttt{genres}, \texttt{publication\_year}, \texttt{publisher}, \texttt{page\_count}, \texttt{language}, \texttt{rating}, \texttt{ratings_count}, \texttt{price},  \texttt{series},  and \texttt{awards}; authors and genres are list-valued.

\end{abstract}

\section{Content}

\subsection{Use Case}

The goal is to integrate book information from three independently maintained
sources. They describe overlapping books but use different attribute names,
formats, and levels of detail.

The integration aims to identify matching books and combine their information
in a common schema with twelve attributes: \texttt{title}, \texttt{authors}, \texttt{genres}, \texttt{publication\_year}, \texttt{publisher}, \texttt{page\_count}, \texttt{language}, \texttt{rating}, \texttt{ratings_count}, \texttt{price},  \texttt{series}, and \texttt{awards}.


\subsection{Requirements Fulfillment}

\subsubsection{Datasets}

Three different datasets are used for this project: a Goodreads dataset from
Kaggle, book data from DBpedia, and an Amazon Books dataset from Hugging Face.
The Goodreads and Amazon datasets are provided in CSV format. The DBpedia data
was retrieved in XML format using a SPARQL query executed in Python. 
The SPARQL query selected all resources of type \texttt{dbo:Book} and returned 20,981 results. 
The exact number of results may vary slightly between query executions.

Table~\ref{tab:overview} gives an overview of the three datasets, including their sources, formats, number of entities, and available attributes.

The project requirements are addressed as follows:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Three different datasets:} Goodreads, DBpedia, and Amazon
    Books satisfy this requirement.

    \item \textbf{At least 2,500 entities:} The union contains 94,823 distinct
    normalized blocking keys, comfortably above the threshold. This is a
    key-based estimate, not a materialized integrated dataset.

    \item \textbf{At least 1,000 entities in at least two datasets:} The
    key-based estimate is 10,397 distinct keys, above the threshold.

    \item \textbf{At least eight attributes in the integrated dataset:}
    The planned integrated schema contains 12 attributes and therefore meets
    this requirement. Books can be identified using a combination of at least
    two attributes. In particular, \texttt{title} and \texttt{authors} can
    be used for this purpose.

    \item \textbf{At least five attributes contained in at least two datasets:}
    Ten attributes of the planned schema are available in at least two
    datasets. Seven -- \texttt{title}, \texttt{authors}, \texttt{publisher},
    \texttt{publication\_year}, \texttt{page\_count}, \texttt{language}, and
    \texttt{genres} -- are represented in all three through the mappings in
    Table~\ref{tab:schema}. Because DBpedia is sparse for \texttt{genres}, \texttt{language}, and
\texttt{year}, fusion by voting across all three sources is mainly planned for
\texttt{authors}, \texttt{publisher}, and \texttt{page\_count}, which are
missing in only 13.5\%, 25.4\%, and 20.1\% of the DBpedia records.

    \item \textbf{List attribute and entity identification:} \texttt{authors}
    and \texttt{genres} are list-valued; matching uses normalized
    \texttt{title} and first-author surname. ISBN is excluded from matching
    and reserved for evaluation.

\end{itemize}

\begin{sloppypar}
The datasets also differ in their completeness. Attributes with more than
30\% missing values are marked with MV (Missing Values) in
Table~\ref{tab:overview}. In the Goodreads dataset, this applies to
\texttt{series}, \texttt{characters}, \texttt{edition},
\texttt{firstPublishDate}, \texttt{awards}, and \texttt{setting}.
In the DBpedia dataset, \texttt{genres} (49.1\%), \texttt{language} (87.6\%),
and \texttt{year} (88.5\%) exceed this threshold. In the Amazon dataset, no
attribute exceeds 30\%; the highest share of missing values is 23.5\%
(\texttt{isbn10}).
\end{sloppypar}

\subsubsection{Integrated Schema}

The source attributes are mapped to canonical names in a common schema (Table~\ref{tab:schema}).
Attributes that describe the same information but have different names in
the input datasets are mapped to the same attribute. For example,
\texttt{author} in Goodreads and \texttt{authors} in DBpedia and Amazon
are mapped to the common attribute \texttt{authors}. Goodreads
\texttt{publishDate}, DBpedia \texttt{year}, and the year extracted from
Amazon \texttt{publication\_date} map to \texttt{publication\_year}; date
precision therefore differs by source.

Table~\ref{tab:schema} shows the planned integrated schema and the corresponding
attributes in the input datasets. The planned schema contains 12 attributes.
Ten of these attributes are available in at least two datasets. The attributes
\texttt{title}, \texttt{authors}, \texttt{publisher},
\texttt{publication\_year}, \texttt{page\_count}, \texttt{language}, and
\texttt{genres} can be mapped to attributes from all three datasets.

The shared attributes can later be used to identify matching books across the
datasets. For example, a combination of \texttt{title} and \texttt{authors}
can be used for this purpose.

Some attributes differ in their representation and level of detail. For
example, DBpedia provides only the publication year, while Goodreads and
Amazon provide a publication date. Such differences have to be considered
during the integration process. \textbf{Examples of heterogeneity.} (i) \emph{Titles:} Amazon titles often
contain series or edition information in brackets, e.g.\ ``One Small Sacrifice
(Shadows of New York, 1)'' vs.\ ``One Small Sacrifice'' in Goodreads, and
capitalization differs (``LETTER TO A CHRISTIAN NATION'' vs.\ ``Letter to a
Christian Nation''). (ii) \emph{Authors:} Goodreads stores all contributors
in one string including roles, e.g.\ ``Terry Brooks (Goodreads Author)'',
whereas Amazon stores a serialized list such as \texttt{["Jodi Picoult"]} and
DBpedia uses a list of author elements. (iii) \emph{Language:} DBpedia values
read ``English language'', while Goodreads and Amazon use ``English''.

The datasets also provide different information about books. Goodreads and
Amazon contain rating information, while DBpedia does not. Goodreads provides
additional information such as series and awards, while Amazon provides a \texttt{subtitle} field whose non-empty values (92.9\%)
mostly contain binding and release information rather than real subtitles. The integration can therefore combine information about a book
from different sources.

\subsubsection{Entity Matching and Overlap}
Candidate works are blocked by normalized title and first-author surname.
Normalization lowercases titles, removes diacritics and punctuation, drops
bracketed text and (when present) subtitles, and ignores common leading
articles. ISBN does not participate in this key; it is reserved for the
evaluation gold standard. The overlap script reports 6,562 Amazon--Goodreads,
2,129 Amazon--DBpedia, and 4,634 Goodreads--DBpedia shared keys, with 1,464
keys in all three sources. In total, 10,397 keys occur in at least two sources
and the union contains 94,823 distinct keys. A manually labelled sample of 20
candidate pairs per source pair contains 60/60 accepted matches (100\%
sampled precision). These are key-based estimates, not confirmed counts in a
materialized integrated database; title normalization can conflate distinct
works, and the sample does not measure recall.
